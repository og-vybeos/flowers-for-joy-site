Our story

It began with a patient thinking about other patients.

Seema Govil was in the middle of her own cancer treatment when she started noticing the people in the chairs beside her — and decided not to wait until she was well to do something about it.

She began bringing flowers into the clinics. Not as a survivor looking back, but as a patient still in it.

She didn't notice she was doing anything unusual. It was only when people kept telling her so that she understood.

During the peak of her treatment she wrote a poem. A nurse read it aloud one day while she sat waiting, and it stayed with her. When Flowers for Joy began, it was the nursing staff who suggested attaching the poem to the deliveries — because coming from a patient who had lived it, the words carried an encouragement no card from a stranger could.

Today that instinct has a name, a team, and a route. It runs every week.

How it works

Flowers to the nurses. Nurses to the patients.

Clinics don't permit outsiders at the bedside — and that's right. So we work with the people already trusted in the room.

FIRST

We gather

Flowers are donated by partner grocers and florists — blooms with plenty of life left in them that would otherwise be discarded.

THEN

We deliver to the nurses

Volunteers bring arrangements to the clinic and hand them to the nursing staff, who know their patients and their day.

FINALLY

Nurses and volunteers hand them over

The flowers reach patients in chemotherapy and radiation, often with the poem attached. Quietly. No branding, no pitch.

A note on flowers and allergies. Arrangements are handed to clinical staff, who determine whether flowers are appropriate for a given patient or unit. In infusion rooms and radiation areas, nurses and volunteers always ask patients and caregivers first whether they would like flowers. We also share flowers with people waiting in labs, follow-up appointments, and other waiting areas — always by invitation, never assumed. Patients or families with allergies, scent sensitivities, or immune considerations should speak with their care team.
